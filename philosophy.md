<div class="page-card page-lead">
  <span class="section-eyebrow">Teaching Philosophy</span>
  <h1>Clarity, challenge, and care</h1>
  <p class="lead">
    I believe that effective teaching begins with clarity, purpose, and strong relationships. Students learn best when they understand what they are learning, why it matters, and how they can improve. In mathematics, I aim to create a classroom where students feel both supported and challenged: supported enough to take risks, ask questions, and learn from mistakes, and challenged enough to think deeply, communicate clearly, and develop true independence.
  </p>
</div>

    <span class="pillar-eyebrow">Clarity</span>
    <h3>Understanding before procedure</h3>
    <p>Conceptual understanding, not just answer-getting. Students should be able to explain their reasoning, justify their methods, and connect ideas across topics.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Explicit instruction and worked examples, used strategically</li>
      <li>Complex ideas broken into manageable steps</li>
      <li>Students know what they are learning, why it matters, and how to improve</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

    <span class="pillar-eyebrow">Challenge</span>
    <h3>High expectations for every student</h3>
    <p>All students can make progress in mathematics when instruction is carefully structured and expectations are high — so learning should be rigorous, accessible, and meaningful.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Tasks that stretch thinking and require persistence, reflection, and precision</li>
      <li>Guided practice, questioning, discussion, and purposeful problem-solving</li>
      <li>Responsibility released gradually, towards genuine independence</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

    <span class="pillar-eyebrow">Care</span>
    <h3>A calm, focused, positive classroom</h3>
    <p>Students learn best where expectations are clear, routines are consistent, and respect is mutual — where they feel safe to contribute and effort, curiosity, and improvement are valued.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Clear routines and consistent expectations</li>
      <li>Safe to ask questions, make mistakes, and improve</li>
      <li>Feedback that is honest and supportive</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="page-card">
  <span class="section-eyebrow">Assessment that guides learning</span>
  <h2 class="plain">Not only to measure learning — to guide it</h2>
  <p>I value feedback that is specific, actionable, and growth-oriented. Every assessment is a chance to move learning forward:</p>
  <ol class="flow">
    <li><span class="flow-step">1</span><strong>Diagnose</strong><span>Identify misunderstandings early, from formative and summative evidence.</span></li>
    <li><span class="flow-step">2</span><strong>Feed back</strong><span>Timely, specific, actionable feedback — honest and supportive.</span></li>
    <li><span class="flow-step">3</span><strong>Next steps</strong><span>Students see clearly what to do next to improve.</span></li>
    <li><span class="flow-step">4</span><strong>Reflect</strong><span>Students become more aware of their own strengths and areas for development.</span></li>
  </ol>
</div>

<div class="pull-quote pull-quote-lg">
  All students can make progress in mathematics when instruction is carefully structured, expectations are high, and feedback is honest and supportive.
